## Introduction to Google Earth AI's Launch and Retraction

According to a report by TechCrunch, Google added a feature called "Earth AI" to Google Earth, but withdrew it just one day after its launch. This feature allowed anyone to create fake images generated by AI and overlay them on actual Google Earth maps. The reason for the withdrawal was the rapid spread of criticism that this feature would lead to the dissemination of misinformation.

The title of the TechCrunch article is "Google nixes its Earth AI feature one day after launch, amid criticism it would spread misinformation," and the speed at which the feature was launched and then withdrawn is a key point of the news. The fact that it took only one day from launch to withdrawal shows how quickly the feature was taken down.

(Source: [techcrunch.com](https://techcrunch.com/2026/07/31/google-nixes-its-earth-ai-feature-one-day-after-launch-amid-criticism-it-would-spread-misinformation/))

## Criticism and Risk of Misinformation

The core of the criticism is the ability to overlay AI-generated fake images on top of real geographic data. According to TechCrunch's summary, "A tool that allowed anyone to generate fake AI-generated imagery and superimpose it over real Google Earth maps quickly spurred backlash," and the problem is that false visuals can be synthesized on top of a "trusted foundation" of real maps.

Map data is often used in situations that require fact-based judgment, such as disaster response, reporting, and urban planning. The risk of introducing manipulable AI-generated content into these areas is considered to be more extensive than the misuse of ordinary image generation AI, which is why the criticism spread rapidly.

(Source: Ibid. [techcrunch.com](https://techcrunch.com/2026/07/31/google-nixes-its-earth-ai-feature-one-day-after-launch-amid-criticism-it-would-spread-misinformation/))
