## Integration of Safety Evaluators and Challenges
Anthropic and OpenAI have expressed their intention to integrate independent safety evaluators within their AI labs. This effort is welcomed by researchers as providing "unprecedented access," but they also point out the need for "transparency," "independence," and "regulation" for effective monitoring. Although specific technical details are not mentioned in the source, it suggests that the role of safety evaluators may be deeply embedded in the AI development process.

(Reference: [techcrunch.com](https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/16/anthropic-and-openai-want-to-embed-safety-evaluators-will-they-really-be-independent/))

## Need for Transparency and Independence
Researchers emphasize that the "independence" of safety evaluators can only be guaranteed if there is "transparency" in their operational processes and evaluation criteria. They also suggest that the introduction of regulations will be necessary in the long term. This argument reflects concerns that voluntary efforts by companies alone may not be sufficient to ensure adequate governance.

(Reference: Ibid [techcrunch.com])

## Possibility of Future Regulations
The source positions the presence of safety evaluators as a temporary measure to mitigate risks associated with the evolution of AI technology, but suggests that the establishment of a legal framework is inevitable. This trend may prompt new discussions on balancing ethical challenges and technological advancements in AI development.

(Reference: Ibid [techcrunch.com])

## Summary
- To ensure transparency in the integration of safety evaluators, developers themselves may need to take action to make evaluation criteria public.
- There is a need to focus on designing processes that guarantee independence and building systems that do not rely on corporate self-regulation.
- By monitoring trends in AI regulation and tracking the progress of future legal developments, developers can prepare to adapt flexibly to changes in the development environment.
