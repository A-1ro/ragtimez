## Announcement Overview: ChatGPT's Free Usage Limit Removed

According to a report by TechCrunch, OpenAI has announced that it will remove the text chat usage limit for ChatGPT's free users and Go users. Previously, the free plan had a limited number of messages, but with this change, text-based chats will be available without limits. Additionally, a new "think button" that responds to complex questions will also be provided to free and Go users, as reported.

This announcement is based on official information from OpenAI, as reported by TechCrunch, and marks the expansion of features previously limited to paid plans to the free tier. This change will directly impact the user experience for free users and is seen as an expansion of features to the free tier, likely in response to increasing competition from other companies.
(Source: [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/08/06/openai-brings-unlimited-chatgpt-text-chats-to-free-users/))

## What is the "Think Button"?

According to the source article, the think button is a new button designed for "complex queries". When users ask questions that they feel are not sufficiently answered by normal responses, they can use this button to prompt deeper processing. However, the official documentation does not provide information on the internal processing flow (such as increased reasoning steps, model switching, or allocation of additional computational resources) performed by the think button.

At present, the TechCrunch article only reports on the existence of the feature and its target user base (Free/Go), without discussing API call methods or setting parameters. For engineers who want to know the specific implementation behavior, they will need to wait for further announcements from OpenAI or updates to their product page.
(Source: [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/08/06/openai-brings-unlimited-chatgpt-text-chats-to-free-users/))

## Target User Base and Usage Start Method

The changes will apply to two user bases: "ChatGPT free" users and "ChatGPT Go" users. The source article explicitly mentions these two names, and the unlimited text chat and think button features, which were previously only available to paid plans, will now be expanded to the free and Go tiers.

The TechCrunch article does not provide specific setup procedures, console operations, or CLI commands that readers can try immediately. Therefore, the only certain entry point available at present is to check the original article, and the schedule for rolling out the features and specific operation procedures for starting to use them are not provided in the official documentation, so we will need to wait for further updates.
